# Glossary
## Key Concepts
### Fire
- **Fire**: [[Introduction_to_Wildland_fire.pdf#page=20&selection=467,0,475,19&color=yellow|Fire is a manifestation of a chemical reaction]] ^c9bb0a
	- [[Introduction_to_Wildland_fire.pdf#page=20&selection=393,0,426,4&color=yellow|(C 6 H 10 O5 )n + O + ignition temperature - C0 2 + H 20 + heat]]
	- The fire begins with an *endothermic* (internal) reaction, that absorb energy. It ends with a *exothermic* reaction, that release energy.
		- [[Introduction_to_Wildland_fire.pdf#page=21&selection=21,0,43,9&color=yellow|The endothermic reactions are known as preignition, the exothermic reactions as combustion, and the point of transition as ignition.]]
	- During the preignition (endothermic reaction), the fuel is brought to ignition temperature.
		- [[Introduction_to_Wildland_fire.pdf#page=21&selection=79,0,100,3&color=yellow|The initial effect of increasing temperature on the fuel is a dehydration process in which the free and absorbed water in the fuel is driven off.]]
- **Ignition**: [[Introduction_to_Wildland_fire.pdf#page=21&selection=161,0,188,6&color=yellow|Ignition is the transition between preignition and combustion, the temperature at which a pilot source ofheat is no longer required. Once ignited, the heat generated by the combustion brings other fuel to ignition, continuing the cycle.]]
- **Extinction**: Is the termination of combustion.
* **Fireshed**: [[00_Wildfire_Risk_Modeling_Oliviera.pdf#page=3&selection=28,45,30,51&color=yellow|the areas with potential to expose communities to fires, according to ignition probabilities and propagation patterns.]]

### Heat and Heat transfer
More valuable than temperature in understanding wildfire events is the quantification of time v temperature relationships. This relationship is conceptualize with **heat flux**.
- **Heat** is a form of energy, in particular, the energy of molecular motion. It can also be described as energy in transit as the result of a temperature difference.
	- **Heat of preignition**: Is the total heat required to raise the temperature of a **unit mass of fuel**. Usually taken to be **320 C**. [[Introduction_to_Wildland_fire.pdf#page=23&selection=754,0,777,1&color=yellow|Reference]].
	- **Heat of combustion**: Is the energy that **maintains** the chain reaction of combustion. Also known as **heat value** or **heat content**. It generally doesn't vary widely in forest fuels, and the value **18,620 KJ/kg** is often used
	- **Heat Flux**: Also called **heat release** or **intensity**, is the **amount of heat produced** per **unit of fuel consumed** per **unit of time**, or **energy per unit area**. [[Introduction_to_Wildland_fire.pdf#page=23&selection=870,0,945,7&color=yellow|Reference]].
	- **Heat transfer**: Is the process by which energy is moved from one source to another. This process is triggered by a difference in temperature between media (elements). [[Introduction_to_Wildland_fire.pdf#page=24&selection=6,0,114,14&color=yellow|Reference]].
		- Heat transfer is key for fires to start. **The mere presence of a heat source does not mean that a fire will start**. Heat **must** be transferred in some way to the fuel. This transfer process is what moves the fire towards the unburned fuel.
		- **Fire behavior and burning pattern is closely related to the manner and rate of heat transfer**.
		- There three mechanisms of heat transfer:
			- Radiation
			- Convection
			- Conduction
		- The dominant mechanism of transfer depends on [[Introduction_to_Wildland_fire.pdf#page=24&selection=135,0,177,6&color=yellow|Reference]]:
			- Fuel arrangement
			- Speed of the wind acting on the fire
			- Slope
			- Direction of the fire is spreading with respect of wind direction and slope
		- **Conduction of heat** is of major importance in the combustion process. [[Introduction_to_Wildland_fire.pdf#page=24&selection=489,6,514,6&color=yellow|Reference.]]
		- **Convection** is the transfer of heat by the movement of gas (like air) and liquid. **Currents of hot air** are primarily responsible for the preheating of higher shrub layers and crown canopy.

## Modeling Concepts
* **HVRAs**: Highly Valued Resources and Assets ^87e658
	* Examples: People, forests, infrastructure, housing, protected areas, agricultural lands, tangible human assets
* **GIS coupling**: [[00_Wildfire_Risk_Modeling_Oliviera.pdf#page=2&selection=160,0,163,55&color=yellow|Coupling simulation models with Geographic Information Systems (GIS) has been a key issue, and it can be done at three levels: loose, moderate, or tight/close coupling.]]
* **Fire regime**: [[Fire_in_Earth_System.pdf#page=2&selection=45,42,59,2&color=yellow|cologists define the “fire regime” on the basis of a range of variables including fuel type (ground, surface, and crown), temporal nature (rate of spread, seasonality, and frequency), spatial pattern (size and patchiness), and consequences (impacts on vegetation and soils) (27).]]
	* 
* **Hazard**: [[02_Global_Forest_Fire_Mihajlovski.pdf#page=3&selection=11,61,13,13&color=yellow|A Hazard is any real or potential occurrence of forest fires that can cause damage, loss, or harm to people, infrastructure, or property.]]
	* The possibility of it happening.
* **Susceptibility**: [[02_Global_Forest_Fire_Mihajlovski.pdf#page=3&selection=13,22,14,85&color=yellow|Susceptibility means the likelihood of the forest or any resource experiencing a positive or negative effect as a result of exposure to fire occurrence]]
	* The effect it will have on the subject.
* **Vulnerability**: [[02_Global_Forest_Fire_Mihajlovski.pdf#page=3&selection=17,3,19,51&color=yellow|Vulnerability in forest fire terminology means the degree to which communities, ecosystems, or assets are susceptible to harm from forest fires]]
	* The degree of the effect.
	* Another definition: [[03_Risk_Analysis_MCDA_Riviere.pdf#page=2&selection=43,1,47,1&color=yellow|The propensity or predisposition to be adversely affected by a hazard, including sensitivity or susceptibility to harm and lack of capacity to cope and adapt.]]
* **Risk**: [[02_Global_Forest_Fire_Mihajlovski.pdf#page=3&selection=19,62,24,14&color=yellow|Risk means the combination of hazard probability and vulnerability consequences (Risk = Hazard × Vulnerability)]]
* **Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA)**: [[03_Risk_Analysis_MCDA_Riviere.pdf#page=2&selection=80,56,87,10&color=yellow|Relates to an ensemble of methods that propose a structured and formal framework for assessing complex problems considering several dimensions. MCDA methods can integrate data of mixed natures, including expert knowledge, and foster collaborative decision making by providing focus to discussion.]]
* **Fire Weather Index (FWI)**: [[04_Deep_learning_Wildfire_Risk_Prediction_Xu.pdf#page=20&selection=90,41,98,62&color=yellow|The FWI system, based on the theory of time lag and equilibrium moisture content, calculates changes in fuel moisture codes (i.e., Fine Fuel Moisture Code (FFMC), Duff Moisture Code (DMC), and Drought Code (DC)) and fire behavior indices (i.e., Initial Spread Index (ISI) and Buildup Index (BI)) according to weather conditions.]]
* **USNFDRS**: US National Fire Danger Rating System
